# DIGI Journal 1 - 07/06/2026

Hello again! The 4 am brain demands another project, and in the depths of my deranged, sleep deprived mind I have come up with an absolutely horrifying idea for the next model in my line of effects. 

A digital effect pedal based on an fpga chip.

I've been looking for an excuse to screw around with fpga for ages, and dsp is one application they're really well suited for. My aim is for this to be a general purpopse-ish design, so I can program a bunch of different effects in the future. 

I've already done a bit of research on parts, and decided what I'm going to use, so I'll just give a quick overview here:

### The FPGA Chip

I've elected to go with the ice40UP5k. There were a couple main factors that went into this decision:
- Its available on jlcpcb pcba
- Its cheap-ish ($8.00, most are more)
- Has a good amount of luts (5k :))
- Is very popular (and thus well supported)

It generally looks like a pretty simple and usable chip, which is very appealling to me.

### Ram

Because I want this to be reasonably general purpose, I'm going to need more than the MBit sram included in the ice40up5k. At a sample rate of 44.1khz and bit depth of 12, that would give me only ~1.5 seconds of audio storage, and I would like a wee bit more for longer reverbs / delays. I've decided to go with the 
APS6404L-SQN-SN chip, which is a 64MBit PSRAM (pretty much sram but a different technology, only used it because it was cheap and for the features I'm about to mention). 
I chose this chip because:
- Storage capacity is good (like 2 mins of audio)
- Cheap (cheaper than sram, thats for sure, still about 3 bucks but thats fine)
- QSPI interface - A lot of sram uses parallel interfaces, but those would eat up way too many pins on the fpga (one i was looking at earlier used 37, the fpga has 48 :O)

### Audio Codec

Unfortunately I can't pull the same trick I did with the rp2040 for audio, and just pwm out, adc in, as the ice40up doesn't have an adc. To that end, I'll need an audio codec, which provides an audio input and output via i2c. I've just gone with the most stocked option on jlcpcb, which was the  . Its a mono audio codec, 24 bit at up to 96kHz which is well beyond what I need. I'm pretty sure its reasonably common, there was a lot of results for it when I searched it up and the documentation seems alright.
